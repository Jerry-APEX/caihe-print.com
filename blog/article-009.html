Screen Printing vs. Flexo: Which is Better for Premium Labels?

If you are designing a label for a premium wine, cosmetic, or luxury brand, standard printing isn't enough. You need texture. You need your customers to feel the label before they even open the bottle.

This is where Screen Printing shines over traditional Flexo or Digital printing.

1. The "Tactile" Difference (Ink Thickness)

The main difference is the amount of ink deposited on the paper.

  • Flexo/Digital: Ink layer is very thin (2-3 microns). It feels flat.
  • Screen Printing: Ink layer is thick (10-50 microns). It creates a raised, 3D effect (like Braille) that catches the light.

2. High Opacity White (No "See-Through")

When printing on Clear Vinyl (transparent stickers), Flexo white ink often looks "milky" or semi-transparent. Screen Printed white is 100% solid and opaque. This is crucial for clear shampoo bottles where you don't want the liquid color to affect the text readability.

Comparison Table

Feature Flexo Printing Screen Printing
Speed Very Fast (100m/min) Medium (20m/min)
Tactile Feel Flat High Build 3D
Opacity Medium High (Solid)
Durability Good Extreme (Outdoor)

3. When to Choose Screen Printing?

We recommend Screen Printing (or a combination of Digital + Screen Spot UV) for:

  • Wine & Spirits: Adding a luxury 3D varnish to the logo.
  • Cosmetics: Solid white text on clear tubes.
  • Industrial: Warning labels that must survive outdoors for 5+ years.
